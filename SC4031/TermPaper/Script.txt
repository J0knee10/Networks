### **SC4031 Presentation Script: Smart Home IoT Protocols Survey**
**Target Duration:** 5 Minutes
**Estimated Word Count:** ~720 words

---

**(Slide 1: Title Slide)**
Hello everyone. My name is Jonathan Low, and today I’ll be presenting my literature review on the communication protocols that power the modern smart home. As we transition toward truly "Ambient Intelligence," the efficacy of these ecosystems is fundamentally tethered to the underlying protocols that must balance device density with severe energy and hardware constraints.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 2: The Problem & Evolution - Fig 1)**
The residential landscape is undergoing rapid digitalization. By the end of this decade, the density of connected nodes per household is projected to reach hundreds. However, we face a technical trade off, the constant struggle between power consumption, data throughput, range and other aspects like security and interoperability. Traditional high-speed paradigms often fail in these constrained-resource environments.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 3: IoT Architecture - Fig 2)**
To manage this complexity, IoT utilizes a three-layer "Edge-Fog-Cloud" hierarchy. The **Edge Layer** consists of the physical sensors and actuators. The **Fog Layer**—composed of hubs and border routers which acts as local coordination to ensure "Local Survivability." Finally, the **Cloud Layer** provides remote accessibility and heavy-duty data processing. 
**(Next)**

**(Slide 4: Wi-Fi: The Connectivity Baseline - Fig 3)**
We begin by analysing Wi-Fi, or IEEE 802.11. Its primary strength is native IP support—allowing devices like smart cameras to connect directly to the internet. However, for a sensor meant to run on a coin-cell battery for two years, Wi-Fi’s high duty cycle and "always-on" radio requirements remain a critical barrier to deployment.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 5: BLE: The Efficiency Specialist - Fig 4)**
In contrast, Bluetooth Low Energy, or BLE, is the efficiency specialist. It spends 99% of its time in deep sleep, using rapid "short-burst" communication to save energy. While traditionally limited by a Star topology to a single room, the advent of BLE Mesh and its role as a "commissioning" tool for Matter has made it indispensable for modern onboarding.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 6: Zigbee & The ZCL - Fig 5)**
Next is Zigbee, a mature mesh protocol built on IEEE 802.15.4. A critical component of Zigbee’s success is the **Zigbee Cluster Library (ZCL)**. By organizing device functionality into standardized "Clusters," Zigbee ensures that a "Turn On" command from one manufacturer is understood by another’s bulb, effectively moving the interoperability challenge to the application layer.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 7: Z-Wave: The Sub-GHz Advantage - Fig 6)**
Z-Wave takes a different physical approach by operating in the **sub-GHz spectrum**. Because lower frequencies have longer wavelengths, Z-Wave suffers significantly less attenuation when passing through typical wall materials like concrete and timber. This provides superior indoor range and zero interference from the crowded 2.4 GHz band used by Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 8: Thread & DTLS Security - Fig 7)**
Thread represents the shift toward IPv6-native mesh networking. Unlike Zigbee, Thread uses 6LoWPAN to give every sensor its own IP address. To secure this, Thread utilizes **DTLS**, or Datagram Transport Layer Security. DTLS implements its own sequence numbers and retransmission timers to ensure a secure handshake over the unreliable nature of UDP, providing bank-grade encryption like AES.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 9: Performance Comparison - Fig 8)**
When we compare these protocols on a radar chart, the trade-offs are clear. Wi-Fi dominates in speed but fails in power. Z-Wave leads in range, while Thread and Zigbee lead in scalability. For a Computer Engineer, the choice of protocol is never about finding a "winner," but about matching the specific constraints of the target application.
**(Next)**

**(Slide 10: Matter & The Hybrid Future - Fig 9 & 10)**
The future is a **Hybrid** ecosystem. We expect Wi-Fi to remain the "Eyes and Ears" for high-bandwidth cameras and microphones, while protocols like Thread or Zigbee serves as the "Central Nervous System" for light weight sensors and actuators like bulbs. The **Matter Standard** unifies these by providing a common application layer, allowing devices to leverage the best of both worlds. 
**(Next)**

**(Slide 11: Conclusion)**
In conclusion, while the IoT landscape remains technically fragmented, the convergence toward IP-based standards and Edge computing is creating a more resilient and interoperable smart home. Thank you for your time.
**(End)**
